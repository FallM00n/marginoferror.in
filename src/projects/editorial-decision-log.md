Most content debates are not new. They are the same three arguments, had again, because the last resolution was never written down. The decision log is a prototype for capturing just enough context that a choice does not have to be relitigated every quarter.

## The shape of it

One entry per decision: what was decided, the one reason that mattered, who owns the exception, and the date. No status meetings, no ritual. The bet is that a visible trail of small calls is cheaper than the meetings they replace.

## What it is not

<details class="expand"><summary>Not a reporting tool</summary><p>If it ever becomes a thing people update for someone else to read, it has failed. It is a memory aid for the team making the calls.</p></details>
<details class="expand"><summary>Not a process</summary><p>No approvals, no workflow states. An entry takes thirty seconds or it will not get written.</p></details>

This is an early prototype. The open question is whether visible context genuinely reduces repeated debate, or just adds one more place to look.
